A nine-phase data project

Could Estonia feed itself?

A bottom-up model of Estonian nutritional demand against domestic production, food group by food group — where the country comfortably feeds itself, where it depends on imports, and what changes if the population actually ate what's recommended.

01 — Headline

Where things stand, in three scenarios

A tonnage-weighted average across food groups with a resolved self-sufficiency figure — not every category, see the coverage note. Individual categories vary far more than this single number suggests; that variation is the point of the sections below.
02 — Production vs. use

Self-sufficiency by food group

Production ÷ domestic use, at today's output. 100% is the break-even line: right of it, Estonia produces more than it uses; left of it, the gap is filled by imports. Toggle between today's diet, a fully TAI-recommended one, and the EAT-Lancet Planetary Health Diet — an international reference diet, population-scaled to Estonia's own energy needs.

≥100% — net self-sufficient 50–99% — import-dependent <50% — critical dependency feed-adjusted lower bound (where computed)
03 — Where it matters most

Critical dependencies

06 — What never gets eaten

Food waste, by stage and by group

Estonia's 2021 national waste study (SEI), allocated to pyramid food groups. Household waste — the largest single share — is a pure efficiency lever: cutting it lowers how much needs to be produced for the same outcome, without growing anything more.
