# Fish and fish product consumption — Estonia, 2024 data (published Jan 2026)

Source: "Kala ja kalatoodete tarbimine 2025 (I vahearuanne)" — interim report commissioned by Regionaal- ja Põllumajandusministeerium, conducted by Eesti Konjunktuuriinstituut.
https://www.agri.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2026-02/uuring-2026-kalatarbimine-vahearuanne-01.pdf

This is a much better fish-specific source than anything else pulled in Phase 1 — none of the PM20/29/31/33/34/37/42/45/47 "ressurss ja kasutamine" series covers fish, and RTU 2014's fish figure is 12 years stale. This study repeats a consistent survey methodology across 2003/2011/2014/2016/2020/2024, so it's also useful for a trend check against RTU 2014.

## Per-capita consumption, 2024
- Resource-based calculation (production+imports-exports-nonfood, divided by population): **17.1 kg/year, live weight**
- Household purchases only (product weight, i.e. as bought, not live-weight equivalent): **~10 kg/year**
- Consumption away from home: roughly +25% on top of the household-purchase figure
- 97% of adults consume fish/fish products at all; share eating weekly rose from 44% (2023) to 56% (2025) — i.e. frequency is trending up.

## Domestic resource balance, 2024
- Professional (wild) catch: 62,500 t
- Aquaculture: 1,000 t
- Total domestic production: ~63,500 t
- Imports: 75,730 t (54% of total available resources)
- Self-sufficiency: "more than double" (i.e. >200%) — consistent with the 2023+ strategy document's "Fish: 300%" figure
- Of total available resources: ~63% exported, ~22% non-food use (fishmeal/feed etc.), leaving only ~15% for domestic human consumption.

## Use for this project
- The 17.1 kg/year resource-based per-capita figure is the right one to compare against RTU 2014's fish consumption figure (23 g/day = 8.4 kg/year, "..kala ja kalatooted", national average) — note these are NOT the same basis: 17.1 kg is live-weight-equivalent (whole fish before processing/filleting loss), while RTU's 8.4 kg is as-consumed product weight from self-reported diet recall. The ~2x gap between them is consistent with a plausible live-weight-to-edible-product conversion factor (whole fish to fillet typically loses 40-60% of weight) plus survey under-reporting, which is a normal pattern for dietary recall data. Worth using the study's own household-purchase figure (~10 kg/year product weight) as the more directly comparable one to RTU — still ~20% above RTU 2014's 8.4 kg, plausibly reflecting a real rise in fish consumption over the 2014-2024 decade (matches the weekly-consumption-frequency trend reported above).
- This gives a genuine, currently-sourced fish self-sufficiency figure (>200%, resource balance: production ~63,500 t vs domestic consumption ~15% of resources) to use directly in Section 5.3 of the model, rather than relying only on the single strategy-document line ("Fish: 300%").
